A temporary deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and prevent a fresh US military offensive against Iran could be announced on Wednesday, with Washington, Tehran and Muscat reportedly close to an agreement after weeks of negotiations. The proposed 60-day arrangement would restore the ceasefire between the United States and Iran that collapsed last month. It is also expected to create an opening for renewed talks with Tehran over its nuclear programme, Axios reported, citing two regional sources and a US official. How the proposed Strait of Hormuz deal would work The draft agreement would introduce separate routes for ships entering and leaving the Gulf. Vessels travelling into the Gulf would use a northern shipping lane passing through Iranian waters. No toll or transit fee would be charged during the initial 60-day period. The temporary arrangement could be extended if the parties agree.
